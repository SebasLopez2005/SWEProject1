## 1. Fitness & Workout Log App

### Initial vision statement

**FOR** beginner and intermediate strength-training participants
**WHO** need a convenient way to record workouts and understand their progress,
**THE** FitTrack application **IS A** mobile-first workout log
**THAT** lets users record daily workouts and exercise sets, review previous sessions, and see historical training volume so they can make informed decisions about their next workout.
**UNLIKE** keeping workout notes in a notebook or general-purpose notes application,
**OUR PRODUCT** connects structured workout records with exercise-specific history and volume graphs, reducing the effort needed to compare performance over time.

### Value to the user and customer

The initial assumption is that individual exercisers are both the users and potential customers. The reason to adopt the product is convenient logging during a workout and accessible evidence of progress afterward. Pricing and willingness to pay remain unvalidated.

### Assumptions to review

- Strength training is the initial focus because exercise sets and training volume are central to the assignment's product description.
- Users enter their own workout data; wearable integrations are outside the initial vision.
- The initial audience exercises independently and wants tracking support rather than professional coaching.
- FitTrack is a temporary project name.

### Questions for the next revision

- Should the primary audience be students, general recreational exercisers, or another specific group?
- Does ease of logging and reviewing progress capture the most important user problem?
- Is strength training an appropriate initial scope for the team prototype?

## 2. Recipe & Meal Planning Portal

### Initial vision statement

**FOR** busy adults who plan and prepare meals at home
**WHO** need to find recipes that fit their dietary preferences and organize a week of meals and shopping,
**THE** MealMap portal **IS A** recipe discovery and weekly meal-planning application
**THAT** lets users filter recipes by dietary tags, assign recipes to weekly meals, and automatically generate a grocery list from their plan, reducing the repeated work involved in deciding what to cook and what to buy.
**UNLIKE** collecting recipes, writing a meal calendar, and preparing a shopping list in separate documents,
**OUR PRODUCT** connects recipe selection, weekly planning, and grocery-list generation in one workflow so the shopping list reflects the meals the user plans to prepare.

### Value to the user and customer

The initial assumption is that the person planning meals is both the user and potential customer. The reason to adopt the product is less manual coordination between recipes, a weekly plan, and a grocery list. Pricing and willingness to pay remain unvalidated.

### Assumptions to review

- Planning is initially for an individual or household, without collaborative editing.
- Recipes have structured ingredients and dietary tags suitable for filtering and list generation.
- Dietary tags support preferences; the product does not promise medical suitability or verified allergen safety.
- Grocery purchasing and delivery are outside the initial vision.
- MealMap is a temporary project name.

### Questions for the next revision

- Should the primary audience be college students, working adults, or families?
- Is reducing planning effort the main value, or should another need drive the vision?
- Will the prototype use a curated recipe collection or user-entered recipes?
